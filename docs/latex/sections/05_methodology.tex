\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Experiment Design}

The experiment follows a full factorial design crossing four base architectures, seven optimizers, and three learning rates, producing $4 \times 7 \times 3 = 84$ unique configurations. Each configuration is trained independently on the same fixed data split. The execution order follows \texttt{itertools.product(ARCHITECTURES, OPTIMIZERS, LEARNING\_RATES)}, iterating architecture as the outermost variable and learning rate as the innermost. Table~\ref{tab:runs} enumerates all 84 runs.

\subsection{Architectures}

Four convolutional neural network architectures pre-trained on ImageNet were evaluated as frozen feature extractors (Table~\ref{tab:architectures}).

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \caption{Base architectures used as frozen feature extractors.}
  \label{tab:architectures}
  \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{l l r X}
    \toprule
    \rowcolor{tableheader}
    \textbf{Architecture} & \textbf{Year} & \textbf{Parameters} & \textbf{Key Characteristic} \\
    \midrule
    VGG19          & 2014 & 143.7M & Deep uniform $3\times3$ convolution stack \\
    EfficientNetB3 & 2019 &  12.0M & Compound scaling of depth/width/resolution \\
    ResNet50       & 2015 &  25.6M & Residual (skip) connections \\
    DenseNet121    & 2016 &   8.0M & Dense connections between all layers \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

Each architecture was loaded via the corresponding \texttt{tf.keras.applications} class with \texttt{include\_top=False}, \texttt{weights=\char`"imagenet\char`"}, and \texttt{trainable=False}. Freezing all base layers ensures that backpropagation updates only the custom classification head, and that the ImageNet feature representations are preserved throughout training.

\subsection{Classification Head}

A common classification head was attached to all four architectures (Table~\ref{tab:head}). The head was designed to provide progressive dimensionality reduction with aggressive dropout regularization to mitigate overfitting on the small dataset.

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \caption{Custom classification head architecture (identical across all 84 runs).}
  \label{tab:head}
  \begin{tabular}{llll}
    \toprule
    \rowcolor{tableheader}
    \textbf{Layer} & \textbf{Units / Rate} & \textbf{Activation} & \textbf{Notes} \\
    \midrule
    GlobalAveragePooling2D & --   & --      & Replaces Flatten \\
    Dense                  & 256  & ReLU    & \\
    BatchNormalization      & --   & --      & \\
    Dropout                & 0.6  & --      & \\
    Dense                  & 128  & ReLU    & \\
    BatchNormalization      & --   & --      & \\
    Dropout                & 0.4  & --      & \\
    Dense                  & 64   & ReLU    & \\
    BatchNormalization      & --   & --      & \\
    Dropout                & 0.3  & --      & \\
    Dense                  & 1    & Sigmoid & Binary output \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The head construction code from \texttt{src/model\_builder.py} is shown below.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=python]
x = GlobalAveragePooling2D()(base_model.output)

x = Dense(256, activation="relu")(x)
x = BatchNormalization()(x)
x = Dropout(0.6)(x)

x = Dense(128, activation="relu")(x)
x = BatchNormalization()(x)
x = Dropout(0.4)(x)

x = Dense(64, activation="relu")(x)
x = BatchNormalization()(x)
x = Dropout(0.3)(x)

output = Dense(1, activation="sigmoid")(x)
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Optimizers}

Seven optimizers spanning adaptive and non-adaptive families were evaluated (Table~\ref{tab:optimizers}). SGD was configured with momentum of 0.9; all others used default Keras parameters except the learning rate.

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \caption{Optimizers evaluated across all 84 runs.}
  \label{tab:optimizers}
  \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{l l X}
    \toprule
    \rowcolor{tableheader}
    \textbf{Optimizer} & \textbf{Full Name} & \textbf{Key Characteristic} \\
    \midrule
    Adam     & Adaptive Moment Estimation      & Adapts LR per parameter using 1st and 2nd moments \\
    Adagrad  & Adaptive Gradient               & Accumulates squared gradients; larger updates for rare features \\
    Adamax   & Max Adaptive Moment             & Adam variant using infinity norm; more stable for sparse data \\
    AdaDelta & Adaptive Delta                  & Adagrad variant with bounded accumulation window \\
    SGD      & Stochastic Gradient Descent     & Fixed step size; momentum=0.9; simple and interpretable \\
    RMSProp  & Root Mean Square Propagation    & Divides LR by moving average of squared gradients \\
    Nadam    & Nesterov Adam                   & Adam with Nesterov momentum lookahead \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

Performance is assessed on the held-out test set (247 images) after training completes. Six metrics are computed for each run (Table~\ref{tab:metrics}). AUC-ROC is the primary ranking metric because it is threshold-independent and robust to class imbalance.

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \caption{Evaluation metrics computed for all 84 runs.}
  \label{tab:metrics}
  \begin{tabular}{lll}
    \toprule
    \rowcolor{tableheader}
    \textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Formula} & \textbf{Notes} \\
    \midrule
    Accuracy    & $(TP+TN)/(TP+TN+FP+FN)$ & Overall correctness \\
    Precision   & $TP/(TP+FP)$             & Positive predictive value \\
    Recall      & $TP/(TP+FN)$             & Sensitivity / true positive rate \\
    Specificity & $TN/(TN+FP)$             & True negative rate; manually computed \\
    F1-Score    & $2 \times (P \times R)/(P+R)$ & Harmonic mean of precision and recall \\
    AUC         & Area under ROC curve     & \textbf{Primary metric} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Specificity is not natively available as a Keras metric and is computed manually from the confusion matrix in \texttt{src/evaluate.py}:

\begin{lstlisting}[style=python]
cm = confusion_matrix(y_true, y_pred)
tn, fp = cm[0, 0], cm[0, 1]
specificity = tn / (tn + fp) if (tn + fp) > 0 else 0.0
\end{lstlisting}
